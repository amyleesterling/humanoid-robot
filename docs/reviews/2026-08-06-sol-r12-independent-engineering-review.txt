[Heading 2] 2. What is good and worth preserving
[Compact] The release ladder HR-V0 → HR-30A/B/C/D/W is the right systems-engineering instinct. It prevents a heroic leap from a rendering to a free-walking machine.
[Compact] The package repeatedly distinguishes stall torque from continuous torque and marks unresolved hardware as SELECTION REQUIRED. That candor is valuable.
[Compact] The E-stop concept includes two channels, monitored reset, two series contactors, external-device monitoring, separate ARM, and no automatic restart. Directionally, this is a strong starting architecture.
[Compact] The walking plan separates Linux behavior from real-time stabilization and hardware energy isolation, and W0-W5 includes test articles, restrained progression, fault injection and fall characterization.
[Compact] HR-V0’s actuator sizing is plausibly feasible: the independent gravity screen gives about 1.70 N·m shoulder and 0.62 N·m elbow static, versus official XM540 stall data of 10.6 N·m at 12 V. This is not proof of continuous life, but it is not fantasy.
[Compact] The repository openly labels the project adult-operated experimental machinery—not a toy—and blocks operation around children.

[Heading 3] 4.1 Interpretation of the torque screen
[Compact] The HR-V0 arm is plausible at concept level. The independent gravity calculation reproduces approximately 1.70 N·m shoulder and 0.62 N·m elbow static torque. Applying the package’s 2.25 screening multiplier gives 3.83 N·m and 1.39 N·m, below the XM540’s published momentary stall torque. Continuous temperature, gearbox life, side loads and hard-stop shocks still require tests.
[Compact] The leg screen is much less comfortable. An 8 kg body with COM only 40 mm from the ankle creates 3.14 N·m static; the package’s ×3 screen is 9.42 N·m. With an assumed 85% external-stage efficiency, XH540 at 14.8 V gives about 14.9 N·m momentary reduced stall. Using the manufacturer’s stall torque/current slope as a rough screen, 9.42 N·m would demand about 3.7 A, roughly 63% of stall current. A 10 kg, 150 mm-offset static case approaches the reduced-stall envelope before dynamics. This is not a continuous-duty design margin.
[Compact] The 1.5:1 stage multiplies motor reflected inertia by 2.25 at the joint before adding belt/pulley inertia and compliance. Rotor inertia and complete link inertias are absent, so control bandwidth and impact behavior cannot be evaluated.
[Heading 3] 4.2 Walking geometry interpretation
[Compact] A 180 × 85 mm foot has half-dimensions of 90 × 42.5 mm. At 0.41 m COM height, the static single-support tipping angles are only about 12.4° fore-aft and 5.9° lateral.
[Compact] The linear-inverted-pendulum time constant is about 0.204 s. At nominal 0.10-0.20 m/s, the velocity term alone corresponds to about 20-41 mm capture-point offset—roughly 23-45% of the fore-aft half-foot length before perturbation, latency, estimator error or safety margin.
[Compact] The geometry is not impossible; it simply demands a quantitative CoP/capture/saturation envelope that the current requirements do not provide.
[Heading 3] 4.3 Power and timing interpretation
[Compact] HR-V0’s 29 A source is not obviously undersized for its three candidates: their summed published 12 V stall current is about 11.1 A. The unresolved problems are protection coordination, DC interruption, regeneration, wiring and tested duty—not raw source nameplate power.
[Compact] HR-30A candidates sum to about 42.8 A published stall current at 12 V. A provisional 50 A source therefore needs a measured simultaneous-motion policy, converter transient model and fault/protection study.
[Compact] Twelve XH540 legs sum to 70.8 A published stall current at 14.8 V. An “80 A-class” tether does not, by itself, define safe selectivity, voltage stability or credible multi-axis saturation behavior.
[Compact] A representative six-joint Protocol 2.0 cycle with one 8-byte command and 20 bytes of telemetry per actuator serializes to roughly 2.74 ms at 1 Mbit/s or 0.91 ms at 3 Mbit/s, before turnaround, retries and diagnostics. Six default 500 µs return delays add 3 ms. The 250 Hz goal is credible only with a deliberately configured high-baud segmented bus and measured harness—not by assumption.

[Heading 2] 11. Final professional assessment
[First Paragraph] Project Button is not ready to build or energize today, but it is also not a mirage. The staged architecture, explicit unknowns and safety-minded gates are a strong foundation. HR-V0 is the correct next physical target and appears technically achievable after detail design. HR-30A is a credible concept. HR-30W is an ambitious research program whose feasibility remains conditional—not yet a design conclusion.
[Body Text] The single most important correction is to stop treating the hosted electrical model, visual rig and requirements prose as adjacent artifacts and turn them into one immutable, native, reviewable configuration. Once that spine exists, the remaining dragons are ordinary engineering dragons: torque, heat, wire, friction, timing and gravity. Formidable, yes. Magical, no.
